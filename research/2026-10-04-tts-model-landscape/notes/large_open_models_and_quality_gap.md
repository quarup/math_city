Every model's GitHub README and `LICENSE` file was downloaded and read directly (primary sources, cited as `github.com/<org>/<repo>`). Leaderboard Elo figures, Hugging Face model-card licences and some paper details come from **web-search result snippets of pages I could not open**; those are marked "(search snippet; page not fetched)". Treat them as credible-but-unverified and prefer the primary README/LICENSE evidence where the two meet.
- The web-search budget for the whole session was exhausted before I could run a last round of verification queries (Spark-TTS / OuteTTS / Step-Audio-TTS-3B / IndexTTS-1.5 weight licences, Raon-OpenTTS, Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS open-weights status, TTS Arena V2 open-weights rows). Those are listed under Gaps.
- "Elo" means different things on different boards: Artificial Analysis Speech Arena scores cluster around 900–1320; TTS Arena V2 (Hugging Face, TTS-AGI) clusters around 1200–1560. Never compare a number from one board with a number from the other.

## Key question 0 — Per-model comparison (organisation, date, architecture, size, VRAM, languages, voice control, quality evidence, licences, failure modes)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the open-weights field splits cleanly in two: a permissively licensed tier (Qwen3-TTS, VoxCPM2, Chatterbox family, MOSS-TTS family, CosyVoice 2/3, Kyutai TTS, Orpheus, Dia/Dia2, Zonos, Parler-TTS, Sesame CSM, Kani, Marvis, Tencent AuK, NVIDIA Magpie) and a "weights open but non-commercial" tier that contains most of the models at the very top of the quality charts (Fish Audio S2 Pro, Breeze TTS 2, Higgs Audio v3, Voxtral TTS, F5-TTS/E2, MaskGCT, Llasa, XTTS-v2). Objective WER on Seed-TTS-eval is now at or below human-read speech for the best open models of either tier (≈1.0–2.3 % English WER vs 2.14 % human), while preference-arena rankings still put every open-weight model well below the proprietary leaders.

#### Known failure modes documented by the model authors themselves

- Dia: "Short input (corresponding to under 5s of audio) will sound unnatural"; "Very long input (over 20s) will make the speech unnaturally fast"; unlisted non-verbal tags "may cause weird artifacts" — [nari-labs/dia README](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia).
- Marvis: "The model might hallucinate words specially for new words or short sentences" — [README](https://github.com/Marvis-Labs/marvis-tts).
- Orpheus: "`repetition_penalty>=1.1` is required for stable generations. Increasing repetition_penalty and temperature makes the model speak faster" — [README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS).
- OuteTTS: repetition penalty must be applied over a 64-token recent window; without a speaker reference "it generates random vocal characteristics, often leading to lower-quality outputs"; accent of the reference leaks across languages — [README](https://github.com/edwko/OuteTTS).
- Chatterbox Multilingual V3 release notes: "optimized to reduce unwanted continuation, repetition, and off-prompt speech, especially in cases where earlier multilingual models were less stable" — i.e. V2 had those problems — [README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox).
- VoxCPM2: "Voice Design and Controllable Voice Cloning results can vary between runs — you may try to generate 1~3 times" — [README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM).
- MOSS-TTS v1.5 changelog lists "better long-reference short-text cloning" and "more stable voice cloning" as fixes, implying short-text cloning was a known weakness in 1.0 — [README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS).
- MegaTTS3: cloning from arbitrary audio is impossible with the official release because the WavVAE encoder is withheld — [README](https://github.com/bytedance/MegaTTS3).
- Kani TTS: "Limited emotional expressivity without fine-tuning"; "Primarily optimized for English" — [README](https://github.com/nineninesix-ai/kani-tts).
- Sesame CSM: "has some capacity for non-English languages due to data contamination… but it likely won't do well" — [README](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm).
- VibeVoice: long-form zh WER 22.6 in Qwen's long-form test (vs 1.78 en) — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS).

### Inferences
- The strongest *permissively licensed* general-purpose candidates for batch pre-rendering English lines today are Qwen3-TTS-1.7B (Apache, voice design + preset timbres + 3-s clone, best English WER in the MiniMax MLS table), VoxCPM2 (Apache, voice design, 30 languages), Chatterbox Turbo / Multilingual V3 (MIT, tags, clone), MOSS-TTS 4B/8B (Apache, pause control, 31 languages) and Fun-CosyVoice3-RL (Apache, best test-hard CER among open models). Parler-TTS is the only one whose *training data* provenance is fully permissive (LibriTTS-R + MLS) but its 2024-era quality is now mid-table.
- IndexTTS-2.5 is usable commercially by a hobby project under the bilibili licence thresholds, but the licence is bespoke (not OSI), forbids using the model to improve other AI models, and imposes downstream notice obligations — it fails the project's "MIT/Apache/BSD/CC-BY/CC0" list even though it is not non-commercial.
- Where the WER tables include a human row, several open models already beat human read-speech intelligibility; the remaining measurable gap is in preference/expressiveness (EmergentTTS, arenas) and in robustness on hard or very short text, not in plain intelligibility.

### Gaps
- Could not open Hugging Face model cards, so weight licences for CosyVoice2/3, Spark-TTS, OuteTTS 1.0, Step-Audio-TTS-3B, IndexTTS-1.5 and Higgs Audio v2 are unverified (repo LICENSE files are Apache-2.0 for all of them, but F5-TTS shows that repo and weights licences can differ).

## Key question 3 — Robustness on short, number-heavy, question-form text ("What is 7 times 8?")

### Takeaway
No benchmark isolates "short arithmetic questions", but three strands of evidence are relevant: (1) LLM-codec TTS is known to hallucinate on extremely short inputs and on repetitive/hard text, which several model authors admit in their own READMEs; (2) EmergentTTS-Eval has a dedicated "Questions" category where the best open model (Higgs Audio v2) beats gpt-4o-mini-tts 55.7 % of the time while most open models lose; (3) non-autoregressive / flow-matching models (F5, MaskGCT, MegaTTS3) and tagged-input designs avoid repetition loops structurally but F5's weights are non-commercial.

### Cited Findings
- LLM-based TTS (VALL-E style) "for extremely short sentences (like a single alphabet 'A')… tend to synthesize words that don't exist in the input sentence, causing severe hallucination problems" — [Improving Robustness of LLM-based Speech Synthesis by Learning Monotonic Alignment (arXiv 2406.17957)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.17957) (search snippet).
- Robustness evaluations on "hard sentences with numbers and symbols" decompose errors into mispronunciation, omission, repetition and hallucination — reported across [RALL-E (arXiv 2404.03204)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.03204) and [Eliminating Stability Hallucinations in LLM-based TTS via Attention Guidance (arXiv 2509.19852)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19852v1) (search snippets; papers not fetched, so the per-model error rates are not captured).
- An EMNLP 2025 paper reports >50 % WER/CER reduction on hallucination-prone subsets by using entropy to detect and mitigate hallucination in LM-based TTS — [Mitigating Hallucinations in LM-Based TTS Models (ACL Anthology)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.976.pdf) (search snippet).
- Dia: inputs under ~5 s of audio "will sound unnatural" — [README](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia). Marvis: hallucinations "specially for new words or short sentences" — [README](https://github.com/Marvis-Labs/marvis-tts). Orpheus and OuteTTS require repetition penalties for stability — [Orpheus README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS), [OuteTTS README](https://github.com/edwko/OuteTTS).
- EmergentTTS-Eval "Questions" category (win-rate vs gpt-4o-mini-tts): Higgs Audio v2 55.71 %; Qwen 2.5 Omni 51.78 %; DeepGram Aura-2 48.21 %; gpt-4o-audio-preview 47.85 %; minimax speech-02-hd 47.32 %; Hume 43.21 %; ElevenLabs Multilingual v2 39.46 %; Sesame CSM-1B 31.78 % — [Higgs README_V2](https://github.com/boson-ai/higgs-audio/blob/main/README_V2.md) (Boson's own table).
- EmergentTTS-Eval "Emotions" WER (short emotional lines): most systems ≤1 %, but Zonos 7.32, Qwen 2.5 Omni 1.22–2.41, MiniCPM-o 12.36, Sesame 17.07, Tortoise 13.04 — [EmergentTTS-Eval-public README](https://github.com/boson-ai/EmergentTTS-Eval-public).
- Seed-TTS "test-hard" CER (the standard stability subset: Fun-CosyVoice3-RL 5.44 best open; CosyVoice2 6.83; Index-TTS2 7.12; F5 8.67; VoxCPM-0.5B 8.87) — [CosyVoice README](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice).
- Qwen3-TTS exposes pronunciation control via pinyin/IPA-style inputs and MOSS-TTS accepts "raw text with Pinyin or IPA for pronunciation control" — [MOSS model card](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/blob/main/docs/moss_tts_model_card.md); IndexTTS-2.5 "improved controllability of Chinese Pinyin, English CMU phonemes and Japanese Kana" — [README](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts). Zonos and Kokoro run eSpeak phonemization and text normalization in front of the model — [Zonos README](https://github.com/Zyphra/Zonos), [Kokoro README](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro).
- Chatterbox Multilingual V3 explicitly targets "unwanted continuation, repetition, and off-prompt speech" — [README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox).

### Inferences
- For fixed, pre-rendered lines the project can sidestep most of these risks by (a) writing numbers out in words in the TTS script ("seven times eight"), (b) padding very short prompts with context or generating 2–3 candidates and auto-selecting by ASR-WER (several teams' RL variants do exactly this at training time), and (c) preferring models with explicit phoneme/IPA overrides (MOSS-TTS, IndexTTS-2.5, Zonos-style eSpeak front-ends) for symbols like "×" or "÷".
- Among permissive models, the evidence favours Fun-CosyVoice3-RL and Qwen3-TTS for stability (lowest test-hard / long-form WER), and Chatterbox V3 / Turbo for short conversational lines; Dia and Marvis are documented as weak on short inputs.

### Gaps
- No source measured WER or hallucination rate specifically on 3–8-word arithmetic questions; the per-model numeric error rates from the "numbers and symbols" hard-sentence papers (RALL-E, attention-guidance paper) were not retrievable.
- EmergentTTS-Eval's "Complex Pronunciation" category (which the benchmark includes alongside Questions, Emotions, Paralinguistics, Syntactic Complexity and Foreign Words) is the closest thing to a numbers/symbols test; the README table columns beyond Paralinguistics were truncated in my fetch, so those scores are not in these notes — the writer should pull them from [the README](https://github.com/boson-ai/EmergentTTS-Eval-public) if the full table matters.

## Key question 4 — Which models support expressive control (emotion tags, style prompts, speed) vs clone-only?

### Takeaway
Inline paralinguistic/emotion tags are now standard in the newer LLM-based models (Chatterbox Turbo/Nano, Orpheus, Dia, Fish S2 Pro, Step-Audio-EditX, Higgs v3, Supertonic 3), natural-language voice/style description is offered by Qwen3-TTS, VoxCPM2, Parler-TTS, CosyVoice instruct and AuK, and explicit numeric knobs (speed, pitch, emotion vectors, pauses) by Zonos, IndexTTS-2.5, Spark-TTS and MOSS-TTS; F5/E2, MaskGCT, Llasa, XTTS-v2, Kyutai TTS and Kokoro are clone-only or fixed-voice.

### Cited Findings
- Chatterbox Turbo/Nano: "`[cough]`, `[laugh]`, `[chuckle]`, and more" native tags; original Chatterbox has `exaggeration` and `cfg_weight` controls (higher exaggeration speeds speech up) — [README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox).
- Orpheus: "Control speech and emotion characteristics with simple tags"; 8 named English voices — [README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS).
- Dia: nonverbal tags (laughter, coughing, clearing throat), `[S1]/[S2]` speakers, audio-conditioned emotion/tone — [README](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia).
- Fish Audio S2 Pro: sub-word `[whisper]`, `[excited]`, `[angry]`…, "15,000+ unique tags", free-form descriptions such as `[professional broadcast tone]` — [README](https://github.com/fishaudio/fish-speech) (non-commercial).
- Step-Audio-EditX: emotion editing (angry, happy, sad, excited, fearful, surprised, disgusted…), speaking-style editing, paralinguistic tags incl. `[laugh]`, `exhale`, `snort`, `inhale`, `chuckle`, `clears throat`, `giggle`; dialect tags `[Sichuanese]`, `[Cantonese]` — [README](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-EditX).
- Higgs Audio v3: "inline emotion / style / prosody control", "inline control tokens for emotion, style, sound effects, and prosody" — [README](https://github.com/boson-ai/higgs-audio), [llmreference](https://www.llmreference.com/model/higgs-audio-v3-tts) (snippet) (non-commercial).
- Qwen3-TTS: VoiceDesign model builds a voice from a description; CustomVoice "provides style control over target timbres via user instructions" with 9 preset timbres across gender/age/language/dialect; "flexible control over multi-dimensional acoustic attributes such as timbre, emotion, and prosody" — [README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS).
- VoxCPM2: "Voice Design — create a brand-new voice from a natural-language description alone (gender, age, tone, emotion, pace…)", plus controllable cloning — [README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM).
- CosyVoice 2/3: "Supports various instructions such as languages, dialects, emotions, speed, volume" — [README](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice).
- Parler-TTS: whole voice specified by a text description — [README](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts).
- Zonos: "fine-grained control of… speaking rate, pitch, maximum frequency, audio quality, and various emotions such as happiness, anger, sadness, and fear" — [README](https://github.com/Zyphra/Zonos).
- IndexTTS-2.5: emotion from a separate reference clip, an emotion vector or text, intensity via `emo_alpha` (~0.6 recommended), speed via `duration_factor` 0.5–2.0× — [README](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts).
- Spark-TTS: "creating virtual speakers by adjusting parameters such as gender, pitch, and speaking rate" — [README](https://github.com/SparkAudio/Spark-TTS).
- MOSS-TTS v1.5: explicit `[pause X.Ys]`, punctuation-following prosody, pinyin/IPA — [README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS).
- AuK: emotion switching, pitch/speed adjustment, accent removal through natural-language instructions — [GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/AuK) (snippet).
- Supertonic 3: 10 inline expression tags, fixed voices — [README](https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic).
- Clone-only / fixed-voice: F5-TTS and E2 (zero-shot clone) — [README](https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS); MaskGCT, Llasa (clone); Kyutai TTS conditions on voices from a provided voice repository — [README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/delayed-streams-modeling); Kokoro fixed voice packs — [README](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro); Kani "limited emotional expressivity without fine-tuning" — [README](https://github.com/nineninesix-ai/kani-tts).

### Inferences
- For "letters from ten citizen characters", the useful permissive combination is a description-driven voice (Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign or VoxCPM2 Voice Design) to *create* ten consistent personas without any reference recordings, then either keep using the design prompt or clone the generated voice with the same model; Chatterbox Turbo adds `[chuckle]`-style tags if warmth matters more than persona variety.

### Gaps
- Tag vocabularies for Orpheus and Higgs v3 were not captured verbatim (README fetch truncated / HF-only).

## Key question 5 — Which run acceptably on Apple Silicon (MLX) or a single consumer GPU for batch rendering?

### Takeaway
Batch rendering of a few thousand short lines is within reach of a single 8–16 GB consumer GPU for every permissive model listed, and MLX/Metal ports now exist for Qwen3-TTS, MOSS-TTS 8B, Chatterbox, Dia, Spark, CSM/Marvis, OuteTTS, Kani, Kyutai TTS and (via llama.cpp-omni) VoxCPM2; the 24 GB-class models (Higgs v2, Voxtral, Llasa-8B) are the exceptions.

### Cited Findings
- mlx-audio (MIT) lists TTS support for Kokoro, KittenTTS, OuteTTS 0.6B, Soprano, Qwen3-TTS 0.6B–1.7B, MeloTTS, MisoTTS/CSM 1B, Spark-TTS 0.5B, MOSS-TTS-Nano, Higgs Audio v3 4B, OmniVoice, KugelAudio 7B, Voxtral 4B, Ming Omni TTS, MOSS-TTS 8B, LongCat-AudioDiT, plus Chatterbox v2/v3 and Dia; quantization 3/4/6/8-bit; Swift package for iOS/macOS — [Blaizzy/mlx-audio](https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio), [MLX Audio TTS docs](https://blaizzy.github.io/mlx-audio/models/tts/).
- MOSS-TTS: "Now 8B model fits onto 8GB GPUs" via llama.cpp GGUF (10 Mar 2026); mlx-audio support (6 May 2026); PyTorch-free llama.cpp + ONNX path — [README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS).
- VoxCPM2: RTF ~0.3 on RTX 4090 (0.13 with Nano-vLLM); llama.cpp-omni on Apple M4 Pro / Metal "RTF ~1.76 (Q8_0)" (slower than real time, fine for batch); `mps` device supported — [README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM).
- Dia: ~4.4 GB VRAM bf16, RTF 2.1× real-time compiled on RTX 4090 — [README](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia); an MLX port exists — [memextech/local-text-to-speech-mlx](https://github.com/memextech/local-text-to-speech-mlx).
- Zonos: "6GB+ VRAM, Hybrid additionally requires a 3000-series or newer Nvidia GPU"; RTF ~2× on RTX 4090; macOS supported via espeak — [README](https://github.com/Zyphra/Zonos).
- Step-Audio-EditX 3B: 12 GB optimal (16 GB "safer"), 6–8 GB memory-efficient, 8–10 GB AWQ-4bit — [README](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-EditX).
- Kani TTS: RTF 0.60 on RTX 3060 12 GB, 0.20 on RTX 4080; dedicated KaniTTS-MLX — [README](https://github.com/nineninesix-ai/kani-tts).
- Kyutai TTS: `tts_mlx.py --quantize 8` example; "MLX: for on-device inference on iPhone and Mac" — [README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/delayed-streams-modeling). Pocket TTS 100M: ~6× real-time on 2 CPU cores of a MacBook Air M4 — [pocket-tts README](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts).
- Chatterbox Nano: 3× real-time on 8 CPU cores; Turbo "less compute and VRAM" than the 0.5B model — [README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox).
- Orpheus: llama.cpp "No GPU" inference path documented — [README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS).
- OuteTTS: llama.cpp Metal backend for Apple Silicon — [README](https://github.com/edwko/OuteTTS).
- Marvis: "Minimum Requirements: 1GB RAM"; MLX command shown — [README](https://github.com/Marvis-Labs/marvis-tts).
- AuK: official MLX branch for Apple Silicon (13 Sep 2026); encoder memory reduced ~7.5 GiB for consumer GPUs — [GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/AuK).
- Higgs Audio v2: "run… on a machine equipped with GPU with at least 24GB memory" — [README_V2](https://github.com/boson-ai/higgs-audio/blob/main/README_V2.md). Voxtral TTS: ≥16 GB VRAM, ~8 GB bf16 on disk — [reeboot](https://reeboot.fr/en/blog/voxtral-4b-tts) (snippet). Breeze TTS 2: "run it on a 12GB card" — [pinggy](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/) (snippet).
- Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-Base is a 4.54 GB download, 0.6B is 2.52 GB — [therundown](https://www.therundown.ai/tools/qwen3-tts) (snippet); a community macOS studio bundles Qwen3-TTS, Kokoro and Dia on MLX — [ch1ppyone/mlx-tts-studio](https://github.com/ch1ppyone/mlx-tts-studio).
- F5-TTS README documents NVIDIA, AMD ROCm, Intel IPEX and Apple Silicon installs — [README](https://github.com/SWivid/F5-TTS).

### Inferences
- A 1.7B bf16 model (~4.5 GB weights) plus KV cache fits an 8 GB GPU or a 16 GB Mac; MOSS-TTS 8B only becomes a laptop option through its GGUF path. For an offline batch of character letters, RTF between 0.3 and 2 is irrelevant; stability and licence dominate.

### Gaps
- No measured VRAM figure for Qwen3-TTS 1.7B or Orpheus 3B was found in sources (estimates: ~6–8 GB bf16 each).

## Key question 6 — Fine-tuning for child voices or accents: published results and data licensing

### Takeaway
Published child-TTS work exists (FastPitch/LibriTTS → MyST fine-tuning, 2022–2023; zero-shot TTS for children's ASR augmentation, 2025) but nothing was found on fine-tuning the current LLM-codec models (Orpheus, Qwen3-TTS, Chatterbox, F5) for child voices, and all three major English children's corpora (MyST, CMU Kids via LDC, CHILDES) carry non-commercial or membership licences that would conflict with this project's rules.

### Cited Findings
- "A Text-to-Speech Pipeline, Evaluation Methodology, and Initial Fine-Tuning Results for Child Speech Synthesis" (Jain et al., 2022): training pipeline for fine-tuning SOTA neural TTS on child speech; a public child corpus was cleaned to a ~19-hour subset for the experiments — [arXiv 2203.11562](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11562) (search snippet).
- "Improved Child Text-to-Speech Synthesis through…" (2023): FastPitch pretrained on LibriTTS then fine-tuned on a small MyST subset; the final model synthesised child-like speech from reference samples as short as 5 s; evaluated by acoustic features and MOS for naturalness, intelligibility and speaker similarity — [arXiv 2311.04313](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.04313) (search snippet; MOS values not captured).
- "Synthetic Speaking Children" (2023) — [arXiv 2311.06307](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06307) (title only; content not fetched).
- Personalised children's ASR using zero-shot TTS (WOCCI 2025) — [Rolland et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/wocci_2025/rolland25_wocci.pdf) (snippet); voice generation for pediatric disordered speech using UltraSuite (Interspeech 2025) — [Rosero et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/rosero25_interspeech.pdf) (snippet). Synthetic TTS data on MyST is used as augmentation for child ASR — [Interspeech 2024 Graave et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/graave24_interspeech.pdf) (snippet).
- MyST (My Science Tutor), LDC2021S05: ≈470 h of English from 1,371 students in grades 3–5 conversing with a virtual science tutor — [LDC catalog](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2021S05) (snippet); the paper gives 228,874 utterances / 393 h with ~197 h transcribed — [arXiv 2309.13347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.13347) (snippet). Licence: "freely available for non-commercial use under… CC BY-NC-SA 4.0"; the Boulder Learning agreement limits use to "non-commercial education and research", with commercial licensing handled separately by Boulder Learning — [Boulder Learning research agreement](https://cemantix.org/documents/bli/MyST-Corpus-Research-Agreement.pdf), [LDC](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2021S05) (snippets).
- CMU Kids: 5,180 utterances of read speech from 76 children (grades 1–3), ~9 h — [Interspeech 2024 Graave et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/graave24_interspeech.pdf) (snippet). (Distributed by LDC as LDC97S63; licence terms not captured — gap.)
- CHILDES-Aligned (Jul 2026): 413-hour general child-speech dataset with corrected utterance timestamps plus a 283-hour quality-controlled ASR subset, built from CHILDES/TalkBank, which is under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 — [arXiv 2607.03670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03670) (snippet).
- Fine-tuning tooling for the modern models exists but with no child-voice results: Unsloth TTS fine-tuning guide recommends Orpheus-TTS-3B (Llama-based, 16-bit LoRA; 10–100 samples of 5–30 s for single-speaker) — [Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/basics/text-to-speech-tts-fine-tuning) (snippet); Qwen3-TTS LoRA fine-tuning repos — [instavar/qwen3-tts-lora-finetuning](https://github.com/instavar/qwen3-tts-lora-finetuning), [sruckh/Qwen3-TTS-finetune](https://github.com/sruckh/Qwen3-TTS-finetune) (snippets); F5-TTS LoRA (~0.9 % of params at rank 16, ~6 MB adapters) — [instavar/f5-tts-lora-finetuning](https://github.com/instavar/f5-tts-lora-finetuning) (snippet); official fine-tuning tutorials for MOSS-TTS (Delay, Local, Realtime) — [README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS); VoxCPM SFT/LoRA — [README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM); Chatterbox single-language finetunes show the family is finetunable — [README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox); Kani TTS pretrained checkpoint and finetune pipeline — [README](https://github.com/nineninesix-ai/kani-tts); Parler-TTS single-speaker fine-tuning guide — [README](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts).
- Orpheus' author advises against synthetic training data for voice finetunes ("synthetic voices lack diversity and map to the same set of tokens") — [README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS).
- Qwen3-TTS CustomVoice presets already span "various combinations of gender, age, language, and dialect", and VoxCPM2 Voice Design takes "age" as a design attribute — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS), [VoxCPM README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM).

### Inferences
- Because every sizeable English child corpus is non-commercial, a child-voice *fine-tune* would inherit a licence problem similar to F5-TTS's Emilia problem; the licence-safe route for child-sounding citizen voices is description-based voice design (Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign / VoxCPM2) or a few consented, self-recorded reference clips, not corpus fine-tuning.
- The 2022–23 child-TTS papers predate LLM-codec TTS and were on FastPitch; their MOS findings do not transfer directly, and the absence of any 2025–26 paper fine-tuning Orpheus/Qwen3-TTS/Chatterbox on child speech is itself the finding.

### Gaps
- MOS / similarity numbers from arXiv 2311.04313 and 2203.11562 not captured (papers blocked).
- CMU Kids (LDC97S63) licence terms and fee not captured; LDC distributions are usually membership/fee-based and research-restricted, but I could not confirm.
- No accent-specific (e.g. British vs American children) fine-tuning results were found.

## Key question 7 — How fast has the open-source gap closed 2023→2026, and what do practitioners expect?

### Takeaway
Measured on the Artificial Analysis arena, the open-vs-proprietary gap narrowed from ≈223 Elo (2023) to ≈81 Elo by mid-2026 and briefly inverted against ElevenLabs' flagship in August 2026 (Breeze TTS 2), before Eleven v4 (Sept 2026) restored a ~100-Elo lead; on objective intelligibility the gap closed earlier (open models at human-level WER by 2025), while on expressiveness benchmarks the best open model still trails Gemini/GPT-4o-audio by ~35 win-rate points. Practitioners now expect vendor-grade quality from open weights but increasingly under non-commercial licences.

### Cited Findings
- "In 2023, the best open-weight model trailed commercial leaders by 223 ELO points on the Artificial Analysis Speech Arena. By mid-2026, that gap is down to 81 ELO — and in blind tests, individual open-source models now beat ElevenLabs more often than not" — [pinggy](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/) (search snippet).
- Early 2025: Fish Speech 1.5 "ranked first among open-source models on TTS-Arena" — [spheron](https://www.spheron.network/blog/deploy-open-source-tts-gpu-cloud-2026/) (snippet); Fish's OpenAudio S1 was "#1 ranked" on the arena when S1-mini was distilled from it (Jun 2025) — [Fish Audio on X](https://x.com/FishAudio/status/1930328792666673353) (snippet).
- 15 Dec 2025: Chatterbox Turbo released; Resemble-commissioned Podonos blind test (2,500 ratings) found 65.3 % preferred Chatterbox Turbo over ElevenLabs Turbo v2.5, 24.5 % the reverse, 10.2 % neutral; comparisons vs Cartesia Sonic 3 and VibeVoice 7B also published — [resemble.ai](https://www.resemble.ai/learn/models/chatterbox-turbo) and [findskill](https://findskill.ai/blog/best-open-source-tts-2026/) (snippets); the Podonos links are in the [Chatterbox README](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox). The evaluations were commissioned by Resemble and are not independent — [oakgen review](https://oakgen.ai/blog/chatterbox-tts-open-source-review) (snippet).
- Mar 2026: Mistral claims Voxtral TTS beat ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 on naturalness in zero-shot multilingual custom-voice tests and matched Eleven v3 on emotion steering — [abhs.in](https://abhs.in/blog/mistral-voxtral-tts-open-weight-beats-elevenlabs-90ms-latency-2026), [communeify](https://www.communeify.com/en/blog/mistral-voxtral-tts-4b-open-weights-low-latency-audio-guide/) (snippets; vendor claims).
- Aug 2026: Breeze TTS 2 at 1,215 Elo vs Eleven v3 1,177, "first open-weight model to clear ElevenLabs' flagship on that board" — [pinggy](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/) (snippet). 28 Sep 2026: Eleven v4 takes #1 at 1,319–1,321 — [progressiverobot](https://www.progressiverobot.com/2026/09/28/eleven-v4-turbo-elevenlabs-tops-tts-arena/) (snippet).
- 2026 vendor-benchmark tables routinely place open models above ElevenLabs on WER: in the MiniMax multilingual test ElevenLabs scores 2.339 English WER vs Qwen3-TTS 0.934 and Fish S2 1.620; on Seed-TTS-eval ElevenLabs Multilingual v2 has the best WER (1.43) but by far the lowest speaker similarity (50.0 vs 65–68 for open clones) — [VoxCPM README](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM), [Higgs README_V2](https://github.com/boson-ai/higgs-audio/blob/main/README_V2.md).
- Expressiveness gap persists: EmergentTTS-Eval overall win-rate Gemini-2.5-Flash-TTS 75.6 % / gpt-4o-audio 72.7 % vs best open Higgs v2 38.9 %, with Chatterbox 24.5 %, Kyutai 21.9 %, F5 17.1 % — [EmergentTTS-Eval-public](https://github.com/boson-ai/EmergentTTS-Eval-public).
- Release cadence of 2026 alone (from READMEs): Qwen3-TTS (22 Jan), MOSS-TTS family (10 Feb) and v1.5 (26 May) and 4B (18 Jun), Voxtral TTS (Mar), VoxCPM2 (Apr), Supertonic 3 (29 Apr), Higgs v3 (4 Jun), IndexTTS-2.5 (10 Aug), Breeze TTS 2 (25 Aug), Tencent AuK (9 Sep) — [respective READMEs cited above].
- Licence trend: the three best-scoring 2026 open releases (Breeze TTS 2, Fish S2 Pro, Higgs v3) are non-commercial; Fish updated its research licence on 7 Mar 2026 to cover the codebase as well as weights — [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/main/LICENSE); Microsoft pulled VibeVoice-TTS code on 5 Sep 2025 after misuse — [README](https://github.com/microsoft/VibeVoice); Meta still withholds Voicebox/Audiobox weights — [aiwiki](https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/voicebox) (snippet).
- Forward-looking signals from the labs: MOSS-TTS 2.0 announced as "coming soon" (29 Apr 2026) — [README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS); Qwen3-TTS README says "other models mentioned in the technical report will be released in the near future" — [README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS); Orpheus roadmap still lists unreleased 1B/400M/150M sizes — [README](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS); Dia2 added streaming dialogue (Nov 2025) — [dia2](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia2).
- Commentary: "the gap between free and paid TTS has basically closed" for self-hosters — [pinggy](https://pinggy.io/blog/best_open_source_self_hosted_text_to_speech_models/) (snippet); an analyst framing open-source TTS beating ElevenLabs as evidence that "API-access moats are disappearing" — [Glen Rhodes](https://glenrhodes.com/prediction-open-source-tts-beating-elevenlabs-signals-that-api-access-moats-are-disappearing-faster-than-most-product-teams-realize/) (snippet, opinion).

### Inferences
- The trajectory is: intelligibility parity (2024–25) → speaker-similarity advantage for open clones (2025) → preference parity with ElevenLabs' *previous* flagship (Aug 2026) → still ~100 Elo behind the *current* flagship and ~35 win-rate points behind frontier LLM-audio models on expressive text. A reasonable expectation is that the preference gap keeps closing on a ~12-month lag, but the subset of weights a free-software project may *use* lags a further 6–12 months behind the best open weights.
- For Math City this means: pre-rendering with a 2026 Apache model (Qwen3-TTS / VoxCPM2 / MOSS-TTS / Chatterbox V3) yields speech that is at or above ElevenLabs on intelligibility and similarity metrics and roughly "ElevenLabs 2024-2025 tier" on listener preference, while the frontier reference (Eleven v4, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS, Qwen-Audio-3.1-TTS-Plus) remains API-only.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) 2026 blind test between a permissively licensed open model and ElevenLabs/OpenAI/Google was found; the Podonos, Mistral and BreezeBlue results are all vendor-run.
- Year-by-year arena snapshots for 2024 and 2025 (which open model led, and its Elo) were not captured because the search budget ran out before those queries.
