## KQ1 (Part A). Which hosted or open products offer voice design from a text description, what do they actually control (identity vs. only delivery/emotion), and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 "describe a voice, get a new speaker" is a shipped, commoditised product feature: at least eight hosted vendors (ElevenLabs, Hume, MiniMax, Inworld, Fish Audio/OpenAudio, Gradium, plus the open Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign and MOSS-VoiceGenerator weights) accept age + gender + accent + tone in one prompt and return a reusable voice_id. A second group (OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts, Google Gemini 2.5 TTS, Azure Dragon HD, Cartesia, IndexTTS2, CosyVoice 3, Step-Audio) only steers the *delivery* (emotion, pace, accent-as-style, dialect) of preset or cloned voices and cannot create a new identity from text.

### Cited Findings

**Systems that design a new speaker identity from text (hosted):**
- ElevenLabs Voice Design v3: prompts describe "age, accent, tone, pacing, and audio quality in a single sentence"; documented attribute keywords are age (child, teen, middle-aged, elderly), accent/nationality ("thick Australian, light French, neutral American"), gender (male, female, gender-neutral); recommended prompt skeleton is "Native <Language>. <Gender>, <Age range>, ..." — [ElevenLabs Voice Design docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-design); [ElevenLabs Voice Design v3 blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/voice-design-v3) (snippets).
- ElevenLabs Voice Design billing: each "Generate voice" click produces three candidate voices and is charged by the character count of the preview text (1 character = 1 credit on v2 multilingual models), i.e. no fixed per-generation fee — [ElevenLabs help center: How much does Voice Design cost?](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voices/voice-design/how-much-does-voice-design-cost) (snippet).
- ElevenLabs policy: "children's voices or voices that sound child-like" may not be added to the Voice Library, and this "applies to voices of minors as well as adult voices designed to mimic or sound like children", justified by the Prohibited Use Policy — [ElevenLabs help center](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voices/voice-library/can-childrens-or-child-like-voices-be-added-to-the-voice-library) (snippet).
- Hume Octave / Octave 2 Voice Design: a voice prompt is "a natural-language description of how the speaker should sound, including tone, personality, emotion, and context"; descriptions can combine "specific accents, demographics, occupational roles" (e.g. "middle-aged, Hollywood movie trailer narrator", "Southern ASMR meditation coach") — [Hume Voice Design docs](https://dev.hume.ai/docs/voice/voice-design); [Hume Octave launch post](https://www.hume.ai/blog/octave-the-first-text-to-speech-model-that-understands-what-its-saying) (snippets).
- Hume pricing as reported by aggregators: Octave 2 at "$0.0000076 per character, or $7.60 per 1 million characters", with plan overages of $0.15/1k chars (Creator) down to $0.05/1k (Business) — [buildmvpfast AI voice pricing](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/api-costs/ai-voice); [aipedia Hume pricing guide](https://www.aipedia.wiki/guides/hume-ai-pricing-for-emotion-aware-voice-apps/) (snippets; the two figures are not mutually consistent and the primary pricing page at hume.ai/pricing could not be fetched).
- MiniMax Voice Design API: "describe the voice you want in natural language, supply preview text of up to 500 characters, and the endpoint returns a reusable voice_id plus trial audio"; attributes named are "tone, gender, accent, energy"; designed voice IDs expire after 7 days unless used in a synthesis call, after which they persist — [MiniMax Voice Design API docs](https://platform.minimax.io/docs/api-reference/voice-design-design); [minimax-ai.chat docs mirror](https://minimax-ai.chat/docs/minimax-voice-design-api/); [fal.ai MiniMax Voice Design](https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/minimax/voice-design) (snippets).
- Inworld Voice Design (launched on X, 2026): "design a voice from a text description, no audio samples required ... generates three voice previews ... Works in 15+ languages"; docs ask for five dimensions — "gender and age, accent or dialect, pitch and pace, tone or emotion, and timbre"; descriptions must be English, ≤1000 characters, and "descriptions under 30 rarely produce useful results" — [Inworld on X](https://x.com/inworld_ai/status/2026703990625362341); [Inworld Voice Design docs](https://docs.inworld.ai/tts/voice-design) (snippets).
- Fish Audio / OpenAudio Voice Design: the `instruction` parameter describes "the voice, age, delivery, tone, accent, pacing, and context", with suggested dimensions "Age & gender, accent, tone & texture, pacing, and mood & context" (e.g. "a woman in her late 30s", "soft American accent", "husky") — [Fish Audio Voice Design docs](https://docs.fish.audio/features/voice-design); [Fish Audio voice design blog](https://fish.audio/blog/voice-design/) (snippets). OpenAudio S1 was "trained on more than 2 million hours" and supports "over 50 emotions and tone markers" such as (angry), (whisper) — [Fish Audio S1 launch](https://fish.audio/blog/introducing-s1/) (snippet).
- Gradium Voice Design (Paris, spun out of Kyutai; launched September 2026): "covers age, gender and vocal texture in five languages, with the regional accents most models flatten into one"; claims "83.4% prompt adherence" on InstructTTSEval English and "over 100 accents from Colombian Spanish to Québécois French or Swiss German"; "free on every plan including the free tier"; a kept voice runs on the normal streaming TTS endpoint — [MarkTechPost on Gradium Voice Design](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/09/gradium-launches-voice-design-write-a-prompt-get-a-brand-new-synthetic-voice-in-seconds/); [Gradium launch blog](https://gradium.ai/blog/launching-voice-design) (snippets). A news aggregator headlines a "72.6% win rate" versus ElevenLabs — [AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/gradium-s-voice-design-beats-elevenlabs-with-72-6-win-rate-for-free) (headline only; methodology not retrievable).

**Systems that only steer delivery/style of preset or cloned voices (hosted):**
- OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts: an `instructions` parameter "guides voice synthesis behavior (e.g. affect, tone, pacing)" and can influence "accent, emotional range, intonation, speaking speed, tone, impressions, and whispering", but the model "is designed around OpenAI-provided voices rather than custom voice uploads", "doesn't officially support explicit accent selection or customization, including a British accent", defaults to neutral American English, and has a 2,000-token input limit — [PromptLayer](https://blog.promptlayer.com/gpt-4o-mini-tts-steerable-low-cost-speech-via-simple-apis/); [OpenAI community thread on British accent](https://community.openai.com/t/british-accent-and-pronuciation-in-gpt-4o-mini-tts/1150812); [Gate.AI spec summary](https://gate.ai/blog/gpt-4o-mini-tts-openai-specs-pricing-api-use-cases) (snippets).
- Google Gemini 2.5 TTS: "use natural language to structure interactions and guide the style, accent, pace, and tone of the audio"; the model picks from prebuilt voices, and multi-speaker "slots" are initialised "with gender, age-bracket, accent and emotional skew" after which "the latent speaker embedding is frozen for the duration of the request" — [Gemini API speech generation docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation); [Google blog on Gemini 2.5 native audio](https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/gemini-2-5-native-audio/); [Cloud Gemini-TTS docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/gemini-tts) (snippets). No evidence was found of a "design a new named voice from text and keep it" feature.
- Microsoft Azure Dragon HD Omni (preview, Microsoft Foundry): "over 700 high-quality voices" with "automatic style prediction using natural language descriptions" and multi-style control; Dragon HD voices are LM-enhanced for context and emotion — [Azure blog: Dragon HD Omni](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azure-ai-foundry-blog/introducing-dragon-hd-omni-azure-speech-new-voice-type-now-in-preview-via-micros/4481288); [Azure HD voices docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/high-definition-voices) (snippets). Identity remains preset-voice selection.
- Cartesia Sonic 3.x: voice library, instant cloning (10 s, up to 60 s "to better retain an accent"), voice mixing, and emotion via SSML-style tags; a third-party integration page lists "voice design with speed and emotion control" but no text-to-identity API was found — [SignalWire Cartesia docs](https://signalwire.com/docs/platform/voice/tts/cartesia); [Cartesia Sonic 3.6 docs](https://docs.cartesia.ai/build-with-cartesia/tts-models/latest) (snippets).
- Sesame CSM (open 1B): conversation-conditioned speech; demo voices "Maya" and "Miles" are hand-designed, not user-promptable from text — [comfyui-wiki news on CSM](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/news/2025-03-03-sesame-csm) (snippet).

**Open-weight systems that design identity from text:**
- Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign (Alibaba, Apache-2.0, released 2026): "describe timbre, persona, age, emotion, pace, pitch, and delivery" in free text; 10 languages (Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, German, French, Russian, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese) plus dialects; six models at 0.6B and 1.7B — [QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS README](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS); [Qwen blog](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3tts-0115) (snippet). A README usage example describes a speaker as "Male, 17 years old, tenor range, gaining confidence — deeper breath support now, though vowels still tighten when nervous" — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/main/README.md).
- MOSS-VoiceGenerator (OpenMOSS/MOSI.AI, 1.7B, Apache-2.0, 2026): "generating diverse voices and styles directly from text prompts, without any reference speech", Chinese and English; instruction-following was evaluated on "gender, age, tone, emotion, accent, and speed" using 160 internal samples; the card warns it is "sensitive to decoding hyperparameters" — [MOSS-TTS README](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS); [MOSS-VoiceGenerator model card](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/blob/main/docs/moss_voice_generator_model_card.md).
- VoiceSculptor (ASLP Lab, Apache-2.0, January 2026): voice design from descriptions like "calm late-night radio host, low pitch, slow pace, slightly husky" with optional fine-grained control of "gender, age, speaking rate, pitch, volume, and emotional expression"; built on LLaSA + CosyVoice2 — [ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor).
- Fish Speech S2-Pro (4B, open weights but under the restrictive "FISH AUDIO RESEARCH LICENSE"): trained on "over 10 million hours ... more than 80 languages"; 15,000+ inline tags and free-form bracketed descriptors — [fishaudio/fish-speech README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fishaudio/fish-speech/main/README.md).

### Inferences
- The product split is clean: "voice design" vendors return a persistent voice_id (ElevenLabs, Hume, MiniMax, Inworld, Fish, Gradium) which is what makes identity *consistent* across utterances; the "instruction" vendors (OpenAI, Gemini, Azure) only re-render preset voices and cannot guarantee a new identity exists at all.
- Pricing for designing a voice is incidental (a few hundred characters of preview text) on every vendor surveyed; the cost that matters is downstream TTS per character, which is covered in the sibling note on hosted services.
- Amazon (Polly / Nova Sonic), Play.ht and Resemble hosted products returned no evidence of text-described voice design in this research; treat them as "not offering it" pending verification (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Could not fetch any vendor's primary documentation page; attribute lists and limits above are from search snippets of those pages.
- No reliable public data on how *consistent* a designed identity stays across many utterances for any vendor beyond anecdotal review complaints (see KQ2).
- Amazon, Play.ht, Resemble (hosted) and Cartesia voice-design specifics: no search results surfaced; not verified either way.
- The Gradium 72.6% win-rate claim's methodology and the Hume per-character price discrepancy could not be checked.

## KQ2. Can any system honor age + nationality/accent + gender + emotion simultaneously? Is "a 16-year-old Japanese boy speaking English with a light accent, cheerful" within reach, and how consistent is identity across utterances?

### Takeaway
Every voice-design vendor and the two open 1.7B models *accept* such a prompt, and benchmarks show the best systems follow ~80–93% of style instructions, but no public evaluation measures fine-grained age ("16" vs "20"), non-native accents such as Japanese-accented English, or child/teen voices specifically; identity consistency is handled by freezing a voice_id or reference waveform after design, not by the description itself.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor prompt guides explicitly ask for age + gender + accent + tone in one description (ElevenLabs, Inworld, Fish, MiniMax, Hume — see KQ1 citations), and Qwen3-TTS's own example uses an exact age ("Male, 17 years old, tenor range") — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/main/README.md).
- InstructTTSEval (2025) is the benchmark used by all 2026 systems; it has "three progressively challenging tasks" testing "both low-level acoustic control and high-level style generalization" (APS = acoustic-parameter specification, DSD = descriptive style direction, RP = role-play) — [KexinHUANG19/InstructTTSEval](https://github.com/KexinHUANG19/InstructTTSEval); [InstructTTSEval paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16381).
- InstructTTSEval-EN scores (APS/DSD/RP): Qwen3-TTS-1.7B-VoiceDesign 82.9/82.4/68.4; Gemini-flash 92.3/93.8/80.1; MiMo-Audio-7B-Instruct 80.6/77.6/59.5. InstructTTSEval-ZH: Qwen3-TTS-VD 85.2/81.1/65.1; Gemini-flash 88.2/90.9/77.3; VoiceSculptor 75.7/64.7/61.5 — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/main/README.md).
- InstructTTSEval-ZH averages reported by VoiceSculptor: Gemini 2.5-Flash 85.4, Gemini 2.5-Pro 84.8, VoiceSculptor 67.6, MiMo-Audio-7B-Instruct 64.5, GPT-4o-Mini-TTS 51.1, ElevenLabs 50.9, VoxInstruct 47.5 — [ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor/main/README.md).
- Qwen reports beating MiniMax Voice Design on "InstructTTS-Eval": instruction following 82.3% vs 78.1%, expressiveness 78.6% vs 74.2%, overall 80.5% vs 76.2% — [Qwen blog: Voice Cloning and Voice Design](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3-tts-vc-voicedesign) (snippet).
- Gradium claims 83.4% prompt adherence on InstructTTSEval English, "outperform[ing] all previously reported systems", and 100+ accents — [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/09/09/gradium-launches-voice-design-write-a-prompt-get-a-brand-new-synthetic-voice-in-seconds/) (snippet).
- Accent control in the "instruction-only" camp is unreliable: OpenAI community users report gpt-4o-mini-tts does not reliably produce a British accent and OpenAI does not officially support accent selection — [OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/british-accent-and-pronuciation-in-gpt-4o-mini-tts/1150812) (snippet); Cartesia needs up to 60 s of reference audio "to better retain an accent" when cloning — [SignalWire Cartesia docs](https://signalwire.com/docs/platform/voice/tts/cartesia) (snippet).
- Consistency mechanism in open models: VoiceSculptor separates "voice design" (description → timbre) from "voice clone" (CosyVoice2 re-uses the designed waveform as prompt) so "a user [can] design a unique voice once and use it consistently for any subsequent text-to-speech task" — [VoiceSculptor paper page](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.10629) (snippet); [GitHub](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor). Parler-TTS instead trained on "34 speakers, characterized by name" to get consistency and reports speaker-similarity of 0.906 for "Will" (Large) and 0.908 for "Jon" (Mini); users are told to `set_seed` for reproducibility — [parler-tts INFERENCE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/parler-tts/main/INFERENCE.md); [parler-tts README](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts).
- Consistency in hosted products: reviewers report "designed voices can drift slightly across very long sessions" and that tone/pronunciation "can shift slightly between outputs" with ElevenLabs — [eesel ElevenLabs reviews](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/elevenlabs-reviews); [toolchase review](https://toolchase.com/blog/elevenlabs-review/) (snippets).
- The Parler-TTS v1 checkpoint relies on named speakers; "Add more features such as accents" is listed as future work — [parler-tts README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/parler-tts/main/README.md). Data-Speech's accent classifier is also still a TODO — [huggingface/dataspeech](https://github.com/huggingface/dataspeech).
- ElevenLabs forbids child-like voices in its library (KQ1), which directly constrains the "16-year-old" part of the target prompt on that platform — [ElevenLabs help center](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voices/voice-library/can-childrens-or-child-like-voices-be-added-to-the-voice-library) (snippet).

### Inferences
- "Japanese teenager speaking English, cheerful" is *promptable* today on Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign (Japanese is one of its 10 languages, so it has heard Japanese phonology), MOSS-VoiceGenerator, Gradium, Inworld, Fish, MiniMax and Hume. Whether the output is recognisably Japanese-accented English and recognisably 16 rather than 25 is **unmeasured** in public benchmarks: InstructTTSEval's APS/DSD/RP tasks score acoustic parameters and style descriptions, not demographic accuracy. A roughly 10-point gap between Gemini (~92) and the best open model (~83) on EN suggests open models miss roughly one instruction in six.
- Fine-grained age is almost certainly coarse in all systems: no training-data pipeline found (Data-Speech, CapSpeech, ParaSpeechCaps, Common Voice) labels age finer than decade buckets, so "16" can at best map to "teens".
- Cross-utterance identity consistency is a solved *engineering* problem (freeze a voice_id / reference clip), not a property of the description model; any hobby project should adopt the same two-stage pattern.

### Gaps
- No published listening test or benchmark for non-native (L2) accent fidelity of designed voices, or for perceived age accuracy of designed voices, was found.
- Hume's and MiniMax's own voice-design benchmark numbers are unavailable (docs blocked).

## KQ3 (Part B). Style control vs. speaker-identity design in the literature — which open models do which?

### Takeaway
The literature separates *paralinguistic* style (emotion, pitch, rate, volume, environment — controllable at utterance level on top of a given speaker) from *extralinguistic* speaker identity (gender, age, accent, timbre — a new voice); most open "instruct" TTS models (CosyVoice 3, IndexTTS2, Step-Audio-TTS, Zonos, Chatterbox, Kyutai, VibeVoice) still take identity from reference audio and only accept text for style, whereas Parler-TTS, PromptTTS/PromptTTS 2/PromptTTS++, VoxInstruct, DreamVoice, VoiceSculptor, Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign and MOSS-VoiceGenerator generate identity from text.

### Cited Findings
- Taxonomy: VoxInstruct frames instructions as covering "linguistic information (spoken content), paralinguistic information (style and emotion such as pitch, pace, stress), and extralinguistic information (speaker characteristics such as age and gender)" — [VoxInstruct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15676) (snippet). The EMNLP-2025 survey "Towards Controllable Speech Synthesis in the Era of LLMs" (Xie et al.) categorises model architectures, control strategies and feature representations and notes the shift "from acoustic-factor control toward natural-language and instruction-based control" — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.40.pdf) (snippet).
- VoiceDesigner (August 2026) defines text-to-voice generation (TTV) as synthesising "speech from both the transcript and a textual description of the speaker voice", distinguishing "speaker traits (e.g., age, gender, pitch) and style traits (e.g., emotion, projection)" — [VoiceDesigner](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13613) (snippet).

**Identity-from-text open models/papers:**
- Parler-TTS (Mini 880M, Large 2.3B, Apache-2.0, 45k h audiobooks): description controls gender, pitch, speaking rate, reverberation, noise, monotony plus 34 named speakers; frozen Flan-T5 encodes the description, consumed via cross-attention — [huggingface/parler-tts](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts); architecture detail from [RepeaTTS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08012) (snippet). Mini v1 was trained on "4 nodes of 8 H100 80GB ... for around 1.5 days" on LibriTTS-R filtered (~1k h) + MLS English — [parler-tts training README](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts/blob/main/training/README.md). Underlying method: Lyth & King (Stability AI / Edinburgh), who labelled "speaker identity, style, and recording conditions" on a 45k-hour dataset — [Semantic Scholar entry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Natural-language-guidance-of-high-fidelity-with-Lyth-King/61c2a081ffbee4b67a296f557aa9baf785a3eae6) (snippet).
- PromptTTS 2 (Microsoft, ICLR 2024): a diffusion "variation network predicts the representation extracted from the reference speech ... based on the text prompt representation", addressing the one-to-many problem; attributes like "low pitched" and "female"; prompts generated with an LLM; Face2Voice extension — [PromptTTS 2 project page](https://speechresearch.github.io/prompttts2/); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=NsCXDyv2Bn) (snippets).
- PromptTTS++ (LINE, ICASSP 2024): "Controlling Speaker Identity in Prompt-Based Text-to-Speech Using Natural Language Descriptions" with a speaker prompt plus a style prompt, trained on LibriTTS-R; code Apache-2.0 — [line/promptttspp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/line/promptttspp/main/README.md); [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08140).
- VoxInstruct (2024): "first multilingual codec language modeling framework" generating speech from a single free-form instruction mixing content, style and speaker (age, gender) — [VoxInstruct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15676) (snippet). Scores 47.5 avg on InstructTTSEval-ZH — [VoiceSculptor README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor/main/README.md).
- DreamVoice (MyShell, Interspeech 2024): DreamVoiceDB annotates "voice timbre ... for 900 speakers from VCTK and LibriTTS"; DreamVG "employs a conditional diffusion model to generate speaker embeddings from text prompts", pluggable into one-shot VC — [DreamVoice paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16314); [project page](https://dreamvoiceconversion.github.io/) (snippets); [GitHub](https://github.com/haidog-yaqub/DreamVoice).
- Chen, Liu, Cooper, Yamagishi, Qian (Interspeech 2024): prompts built from *listener impressions*; LoRA-adapted LM extracts speaker traits; "separate the prompt-to-speaker module from the multi-speaker TTS system" with both a discriminative mapping and a flow-matching generative variant — [ISCA Archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/chen24q_interspeech.html) (snippet).
- VoiceSculptor (2026): "jointly processes the instruction text, structured attribute tokens (like 'middle-aged', 'happy', 'high pitch'), and speech tokens in a unified autoregressive framework" — [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.10629) (snippet).
- MOSS-VoiceGenerator (2026): "voice description instructions and the text to be synthesized are concatenated and jointly tokenized as input" — [model card](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/blob/main/docs/moss_voice_generator_model_card.md).
- Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign (2026): see KQ1/KQ2.
- Audiobox (Meta, 2023): "describe-and-generate" speech from "A young woman speaks with a high pitch and fast pace", plus dual voice+text restyling — [Meta AI blog](https://ai.meta.com/blog/audiobox-generating-audio-voice-natural-language-prompts/) (snippet). Weight-release status not verified (Gap).
- CapSpeech (2025, CC BY-NC 4.0): benchmark with "over 10 million machine-annotated" and "nearly 0.36 million human-annotated" audio–caption pairs; tasks CapTTS, CapTTS-SE, AccCapTTS (accent), EmoCapTTS, AgentTTS; captions cover age, gender, accent, emotion; built on Parler-TTS, F5-TTS, SSR-Speech, Data-Speech, EzAudio and Vox-Profile; pretraining improved Style-ACC by 13.9–26.7% (AR) and 19.6–27.2% (NAR) — [WangHelin1997/CapSpeech](https://github.com/WangHelin1997/CapSpeech); [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02863) (snippet).
- Other 2026 identity-design papers found (abstract-level only): CapTalk "Unified Voice Design for Single-Utterance and Dialogue Speech Generation" — [arXiv 2604.08363](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08363); ProPS "Prompted Profile Synthesis for Natural Language-Conditioned Speaker Embedding Distributions" — [arXiv 2607.05276](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05276); MoE-TTS for out-of-domain descriptions — [arXiv 2508.11326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11326); HiStyle hierarchical style-embedding predictor — [arXiv 2509.25842](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25842).

**Style-only (identity from audio) open models:**
- CosyVoice 3 (0.5B, Apache-2.0): natural-language instruction + `<|endofprompt|>` prepended to text; supports "languages, dialects, emotions, speed, volume"; fine-grained tags like `[laughter]` — [FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice); [CosyVoice 3 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v1) (snippet). Identity comes from prompt speech (inference; not explicitly stated in README).
- IndexTTS2: "Speaker identity requires reference audio"; emotion controlled via reference clip, 8-dim vector, or text through a fine-tuned Qwen3-0.6B; "bilibili Model Use License Agreement" — [index-tts/index-tts README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/index-tts/index-tts/main/README.md).
- Step-Audio-TTS-3B: instruction control of emotions, dialects (Cantonese, Sichuanese), speed, rap/singing; 8 GB GPU minimum; weights under their own licence, code Apache-2.0 — [stepfun-ai/Step-Audio](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio/main/README.md).
- Zonos-v0.1 (1.6B, 200k h, Apache-2.0): conditions on "speaker embedding or audio prefix" plus speaking rate, pitch variation, audio quality and emotion vector; no text identity — [Zyphra/Zonos](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Zyphra/Zonos/main/README.md); licence from [HF README](https://huggingface.co/Zyphra/Zonos-v0.1-transformer) (snippet).
- Chatterbox (Resemble; MIT; Turbo 350M, Nano 110M, Multilingual 500M): ~10 s reference cloning, `exaggeration` knob, paralinguistic tags, Perth watermark on every output; "No text-based voice identity description supported" — [resemble-ai/chatterbox](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/main/README.md).
- Kyutai TTS 1.6B / Pocket TTS 100M: voices via precomputed embeddings (Pocket TTS also clones from a wav); no text description capability — [kyutai-labs/delayed-streams-modeling](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kyutai-labs/delayed-streams-modeling/main/README.md); [kyutai-labs/pocket-tts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts/main/README.md).
- Microsoft VibeVoice (TTS 1.5B, Realtime 0.5B): multi-speaker long-form from reference voices; "not recommend[ed] ... in commercial or real-world applications without further testing" — [microsoft/VibeVoice](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/VibeVoice/main/README.md).
- MiMo-Audio-7B-Instruct: "open-source SOTA on ... instruct-TTS evaluations" (InstructTTSEval EN 80.6/77.6/59.5 per Qwen's table) — [XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-Audio](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-Audio/main/README.md); Kimi-Audio-7B (13M h) lists "speed control, accent control, emotion control, style control" in conversation — [MoonshotAI/Kimi-Audio](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-Audio/master/README.md); Baichuan-Audio (Apache-2.0) exposes no voice/style controls — [baichuan-inc/Baichuan-Audio](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/baichuan-inc/Baichuan-Audio/main/README.md).
- VoiceLDM (Apache-2.0): the description prompt "controls environmental context rather than speaker characteristics" ("She is talking in a park"); trained on AudioSet, CommonVoice 13 English, VoxCeleb1, DEMAND with CLAP + Whisper — [glory20h/VoiceLDM](https://github.com/glory20h/VoiceLDM).

### Inferences
- The clean research distinction is between (i) *style prompting* — the speaker is given, text adjusts prosody/emotion — and (ii) *voice design / text-to-voice (TTV)* — text must produce a plausible new speaker. Only (ii) solves the user's problem. Large general "audio LLMs" (MiMo, Kimi, Step) get partial identity control as a by-product of scale, while purpose-built TTV systems (Qwen3-TTS-VD, MOSS-VG, VoiceSculptor) now beat them.
- Among permissively licensed open TTV weights usable in a shipped free product, only Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign (Apache-2.0), MOSS-VoiceGenerator (Apache-2.0), VoiceSculptor (Apache-2.0) and Parler-TTS (Apache-2.0) qualify; CapSpeech and ParaSpeechCaps checkpoints are non-commercial; Fish S2-Pro and IndexTTS2 have bespoke licences.

### Gaps
- Full texts of CosyVoice 3, VoxInstruct, PromptTTS 1/2, InstructTTS, Audiobox, CapTalk, ProPS, VoiceDesigner, MINT-Bench, Poly-InstructTTS, FlexiVoice and the EMNLP survey were unreachable; architecture details are from abstracts/snippets.
- Audiobox: whether weights were ever released could not be verified (my recollection is demo-only, unverified).
- Hume Octave, MiniMax, Inworld, Gradium model architectures are undisclosed.

## KQ4. How do description-conditioned models encode the description, and what are the known weaknesses (mode collapse, poor fine-grained age, accent leakage)?

### Takeaway
Three encoding patterns exist — (a) frozen text encoder → cross-attention into an AR codec LM (Parler-TTS), (b) description tokens concatenated with the transcript into a single LLM stream (MOSS-VG, VoiceSculptor, Qwen3-TTS, VoxInstruct), and (c) description → predicted speaker embedding via diffusion/flow (PromptTTS 2, DreamVG, Chen et al., ProPS); the known failure modes are the one-to-many ambiguity (many voices fit one description), low attribute coverage in training captions (accent and fine age largely absent), and strong coupling to a few named/dominant speakers.

### Cited Findings
- Pattern (a): Parler-TTS "frames speech synthesis as autoregressive generation of discrete audio tokens, jointly conditioned on a transcript and a natural-language style description. A frozen Flan-T5 encoder processes the style description via cross-attention layers" — [RepeaTTS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08012) (snippet). The transcript "is simply passed through an embedding layer and concatenated to the decoder input hidden states" — [parler-tts training README](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts/blob/main/training/README.md).
- Pattern (b): MOSS-VoiceGenerator concatenates and jointly tokenises description and text — [model card](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/blob/main/docs/moss_voice_generator_model_card.md); VoiceSculptor jointly processes instruction text, attribute tokens and speech tokens — [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.10629) (snippet); CosyVoice 3 prepends the instruction and `<|endofprompt|>` to the text — [CosyVoice 3 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v1) (snippet).
- Pattern (c): PromptTTS 2's variation network predicts the reference-speech representation from the prompt because the prompt underspecifies the voice — [PromptTTS 2](https://speechresearch.github.io/prompttts2/) (snippet); DreamVG generates speaker embeddings with a conditional diffusion model — [DreamVoice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16314) (snippet); Chen et al. offer both discriminative mapping and flow-matching generation of speaker embeddings from impression prompts — [ISCA](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/chen24q_interspeech.html) (snippet); ProPS models "natural language-conditioned speaker embedding distributions" — [arXiv 2607.05276](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05276).
- Caption coverage weakness: Data-Speech (the Parler-TTS annotation pipeline) computes only speaking rate, SNR, C50 reverberation, pitch mean/std, PESQ/SI-SDR and monotony, takes gender from dataset metadata, and lists "Accent classification" under TODO — [huggingface/dataspeech](https://github.com/huggingface/dataspeech). Parler-TTS lists accents as future work — [parler-tts README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/parler-tts/main/README.md).
- Rich tags only recently scaled: before ParaSpeechCaps, "existing large-scale datasets only cover basic tags (e.g. low-pitched, slow, loud)"; PSC adds 59 tags (e.g. guttural, nasal, whispered) over 282 h human + 2,450 h automatic — [ParaSpeechCaps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04713); [GitHub](https://github.com/ajd12342/paraspeechcaps).
- Speaker-consistency hack and its cost: Parler-TTS's consistency comes from 34 named speakers, with top speakers differing per checkpoint (Will 0.906 vs Jon 0.908 similarity) — [INFERENCE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/parler-tts/main/INFERENCE.md). A 2026 paper on Greek Parler-style TTS is titled "Deterministic Prompting for Speaker-Stable Low-Resource ... TTS", indicating speaker instability of description-only prompting remains an active problem — [arXiv 2609.10022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.10022) (title only).
- Out-of-domain descriptions are a recognised failure: MoE-TTS targets "Out-of-Domain Text Understanding for Description-based TTS" — [arXiv 2508.11326](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11326) (title); VoiceSculptor adds RAG-based "command refinement" to rewrite user prompts into the training distribution — [GitHub](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor).
- Decoding sensitivity: MOSS-VoiceGenerator "is sensitive to decoding hyperparameters" (recommended audio_temperature 1.5, top_p 0.6, top_k 50, repetition_penalty 1.1) — [model card](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/blob/main/docs/moss_voice_generator_model_card.md).
- Accent-without-data direction: "Accent Vector: Controllable Accent Manipulation for Multilingual TTS Without Accented Data" (2026) — [arXiv 2603.07534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07534) (title); earlier AccentBox did zero-shot accent generation — [arXiv 2409.09098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.09098) (title).
- Benchmark ceiling: even the best open model trails Gemini by ~10 points on InstructTTSEval-EN DSD (82.4 vs 93.8) — [Qwen3-TTS README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/main/README.md).

### Inferences
- "Mode collapse to a few voices" is structurally encouraged by pattern (a)/(b) training on data where only ~tens of speakers are labelled richly (Parler's 34 names; PSC-Base's human labels over 282 h): the model learns to snap descriptions onto the nearest well-covered speaker. Pattern (c) (generate an embedding distribution, then sample) is the literature's answer, which is why 2026 work (ProPS, VoiceDesigner, Chen et al.) centres on embedding-space generation with explicit diversity.
- Fine-grained age cannot be learned if no corpus labels it finer than decades (Common Voice buckets, EARS 18–75 adults, CapSpeech "age" captions) — see KQ5. Expect "teen / young adult / middle-aged / elderly" resolution at best.
- "Accent leakage" (asking for Japanese-accented English and getting Japanese-language phonetics or a generic Asian stereotype) is plausible because accent labels in open training data are sparse and self-reported; no paper found measures it.

### Gaps
- No paper found that quantitatively reports mode-collapse/diversity metrics for description-conditioned TTS (likely exists inside VoiceDesigner/ProPS but those full texts were unreachable).
- No study found of age-estimation accuracy on designed voices.

## KQ5 (Part C data). What labelled data exists for age, accent and nationality at scale, with what licences, and are automatic captioning pipelines how people get descriptions?

### Takeaway
The only large, permissively licensed source with age + gender + self-reported accent is Mozilla Common Voice (CC0; English ≈2,827 validated hours from ~100k speakers, ~64% age-labelled, ~6% teens), optionally cleaned via GLOBE; every dedicated accent corpus (L2-ARCTIC, Speech Accent Archive, UME-ERJ) and every children's corpus (MyST, CMU Kids) is non-commercial or paywalled, and L2-ARCTIC has no Japanese speakers; rich *descriptions* are universally produced by automatic pipelines (Data-Speech, ParaSpeechCaps, CapSpeech) rather than human writing.

### Cited Findings

**Common Voice (CC0):**
- Released "under Creative Commons CC0 license"; contributors "can opt-in to provide metadata like their age, sex, and accent" — [Mozilla blog](https://blog.mozilla.org/en/mozilla/news/sharing-our-common-voices-mozilla-releases-the-largest-to-date-public-domain-transcribed-voice-dataset/); fields include `accent, age, client_id, gender, locale` — [HF card common_voice_12_0](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mozilla-foundation/common_voice_12_0) (snippets).
- Latest releases "as of 2026-09 are v27.0 for Scripted Speech (295 languages) and v5.0 for Spontaneous Speech (80 languages)" — [common-voice/cv-dataset README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/common-voice/cv-dataset/main/README.md).
- English in cv-corpus-27.0 (2026-09-11): 3,795.9 total h, 2,826.8 validated h, 100,546 speakers; age distribution: no age 35.58%, teens 5.89%, twenties 24.81%, thirties 13.82%, fourties 9.34%, fifties 5.28%, sixties 4.50%, seventies 0.68%; gender: unspecified 38.94%, male 43.18%, female 17.76% — [cv-corpus-27.0 stats JSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/common-voice/cv-dataset/main/datasets/scripted-speech/cv-corpus-27.0-2026-09-11.json). (The fetch tool's "overall totals" line for all locales was internally inconsistent and is omitted.)
- GLOBE (Interspeech 2024) derives from Common Voice: "23,519 speakers and covers 164 accents worldwide", with filtering/enhancement and "populating all missing speaker metadata"; licence shown on HF only as a custom "Globe" licence — [GLOBE paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/wang24b_interspeech.pdf); [HF GLOBE_V2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MushanW/GLOBE_V2) (snippets).

**Accent / L2 corpora:**

- UME-ERJ (English Read by Japanese): 202 speakers (100 M, 102 F), phonemic + prosodic sets; "available for research purpose only", non-commercial, distributed by NII Speech Resources Consortium / ELRA — [NII SRC UME-ERJ](https://research.nii.ac.jp/src/en/UME-ERJ.html); [ELRA entry](http://universal.elra.info/product_info.php?cPath=37_39&products_id=1788) (snippets).
- AESRC2020 (Accented English Speech Recognition Challenge): 160 h training set, 8 accents at 20 h each including Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Russian, Indian, Portuguese, Spanish, Canadian (+UK/US), 16 kHz with transcripts — [AESRC2020 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.10233) (snippet). Licence/redistribution terms not found (Gap).
- VCTK: "109 native English speakers from different regions, such as the US and the UK" — [GLOBE paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14875v1) (snippet). Licence page unreachable (Gap; widely cited as CC BY 4.0 but unverified here).
- VoxCeleb: "speech from speakers of 145 different nationalities"; nationality "crawled from Wikipedia ... country of citizenship, and not ethnicity"; VoxCeleb2 metadata CC BY-SA 4.0; audio "available to download for research purposes under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License" (YouTube-sourced) — [VoxCeleb2 page](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/data/voxceleb/vox2.html); [VoxCeleb paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0885230819302712) (snippets). A follow-up adds age/gender labels — [VoxCeleb Enrichment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.13510) (title).

**Children's speech:**
- MyST: "approximately 400 hours of conversational speech, spanning some 230K utterances across about 10.5K virtual tutor sessions by around 1.3K third, fourth and fifth grade students"; 100K utterances transcribed; "freely available for non-commercial use ... (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" with a separate commercial licence — [MyST paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.13347); [LREC 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.1052/) (snippets).

**Expressive / captioned corpora used for description-TTS:**
- ParaSpeechCaps: 282 h human-labelled (PSC-Base) + 2,450 h auto-labelled (PSC-Scaled), 59 tags "spanning speaker-level intrinsic style tags and utterance-level situational style tags"; dataset and models CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, code MIT; fine-tuned Parler-TTS gains +7.9% Consistency MOS and +15.5% Naturalness MOS — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04713); [GitHub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ajd12342/paraspeechcaps/main/README.md).

- EARS: "100 h of speech data from 107 speakers", 48 kHz anechoic, metadata "age, ethnicity, gender, weight, height, native language", ages 18–75, 7 reading styles + 22 emotions; "CC-NC 4.0" — [facebookresearch/ears_dataset](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/ears_dataset/main/README.md).
- Expresso: CC BY-NC 4.0 — [HF ylacombe/expresso](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ylacombe/expresso) (snippet).
- Emilia: 101.7k h CC BY-NC 4.0; Emilia-YODAS 113.9k h CC BY 4.0; six languages incl. Japanese; no speaker demographics yet (gender/age/accent listed as TODO) — [Amphion Emilia README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-mmlab/Amphion/main/preprocessors/Emilia/README.md).
- LibriTTS-R: 585 h, 2,456 speakers, 24 kHz (licence for -R not confirmed; original LibriTTS CC BY 4.0) — [HF libritts_r_filtered / libritts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/parler-tts/libritts_r_filtered) (snippet).
- Jenny (Dioco): single speaker; commercial use permitted with "Jenny"/"Jenny (Dioco)" attribution in the generating software — [Kaggle Jenny dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/noml4u/jenny-tts-dataset) (snippet).
- TextrolSpeech: labels gender, pitch, tempo, energy, emotion (no age) over LibriTTS + VCTK sources — [jishengpeng/TextrolSpeech](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jishengpeng/TextrolSpeech/main/README.md).
- DreamVoiceDB: human timbre annotations for 900 VCTK + LibriTTS speakers — [DreamVoice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16314) (snippet).

**Captioning pipelines:**
- Data-Speech (MIT): "1) annotate the dataset with continuous variables ... 2) map those annotations to text bins ... 3) create natural language descriptions" using Mistral-7B or Llama-3-8B; v2 (Aug 2024) added PESQ and SI-SDR — [huggingface/dataspeech](https://github.com/huggingface/dataspeech).
- ParaSpeechCaps "combine[s] off-the-shelf text and speech embedders, classifiers and an audio language model to automatically scale rich tag annotations" — [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04713) (snippet). CapSpeech uses Vox-Profile among others — [GitHub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WangHelin1997/CapSpeech/main/README.md). Lyth & King's whole contribution was replacing human captions with synthetic annotations at 45k h scale — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Natural-language-guidance-of-high-fidelity-with-Lyth-King/61c2a081ffbee4b67a296f557aa9baf785a3eae6) (snippet).

### Inferences
- For a *shipped* free product (CC-BY-NC excluded), the usable English training pool with demographic labels is essentially Common Voice (+GLOBE if its custom licence permits), Emilia-YODAS (no demographics), LibriTTS(-R) (no age/accent), VoxCeleb audio (CC BY 4.0 nominally, but YouTube-derived and "for research purposes" — legally murky for a shipped model), and Jenny. For a *learning* project, MyST, EARS, Expresso, L2-ARCTIC, ParaSpeechCaps and CapSpeech are all fine.
- Japanese-accented English: Common Voice English's free-text `accent` field is the only permissive source and the count of "Japanese" self-labels is unknown (estimate: at most a few hundred speakers — unverified). UME-ERJ (202 speakers) is the best research-only resource. AESRC has 20 h but unclear terms.
- Children/teens: Common Voice "teens" ≈5.9% of ~2,827 validated English hours ≈ 165 h (estimate, assumes age mix is uniform over hours), largely 13–19-year-olds, self-recorded; this is the only permissive teen data found. Pre-teen voices (6–12) have no permissive source.
- Nobody writes descriptions by hand at scale; the standard recipe is metadata + acoustic measurements + classifier tags → LLM paraphrase into a sentence. A hobbyist should reuse Data-Speech and add age/accent fields from Common Voice metadata rather than annotate.

### Gaps
- Exact licences for VCTK, LibriTTS-R, GLOBE and AESRC2020 not verified (pages blocked).
- Number of Common Voice English speakers self-reporting a Japanese accent: not retrievable here (needs a local pass over `validated.tsv`).
- Whether CapSpeech's AccCapTTS subset includes Japanese-accented English and which corpus it draws on: unknown.

## KQ6. Ethics and safety: designing child-sounding voices and accent stereotyping — how do vendors and researchers handle it?

### Takeaway
Vendors handle child voices by prohibition (ElevenLabs bans child-like voices outright) and misuse by watermarking/disclaimers (Chatterbox, VoiceSculptor, VibeVoice), while 2025–2026 HCI/FAccT research documents that commercial TTS reproduces accent hierarchies (white, male, US-accented = "competent") and performs worse on marked accents — so a "Japanese-accented teen" voice carries both a child-safety and a stereotyping risk that a hobby project must consciously design around.

### Cited Findings
- ElevenLabs: child or child-like voices (including adult imitations) are barred from the Voice Library to "safeguard against potential misuse of voices that could pose risks to children" — [ElevenLabs help center](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/voices/voice-library/can-childrens-or-child-like-voices-be-added-to-the-voice-library) (snippet).
- Chatterbox embeds Resemble's Perth watermark in every output, surviving MP3 compression with "nearly 100% detection accuracy" — [resemble-ai/chatterbox](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/main/README.md). VoiceSculptor's licence note prohibits "unauthorized voice cloning, impersonation, fraud, scams, deepfakes" — [ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor](https://github.com/ASLP-lab/VoiceSculptor). VibeVoice warns "High-quality synthetic speech can be misused to create convincing fake audio content" and is research-only — [microsoft/VibeVoice](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/VibeVoice/main/README.md). Hume markets voice design as a way to "avoid cloning consent issues for fictional characters" — [top5apps Hume review](https://top5apps.ai/best-ai-apps/best-ai-voice-generators/hume-ai/) (snippet).
- "The Sound of Silencing: Identities and Ideologies in Commercial Text-To-Speech" finds a leading commercial TTS "disproportionately reproduces white, male, US-accented speech when prompted to convey competence" — [ACM DOI 10.1145/3772363.3798657](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798657) (snippet).
- "'It's not a representation of me': Examining Accent Bias and Digital Exclusion in Synthetic AI Voice Services" (FAccT 2025) evaluates Speechify and ElevenLabs across five English accents and finds "technical performance disparities" that "may inadvertently reinforce linguistic privilege and accent-based discrimination" — [arXiv 2504.09346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09346) (snippet).
- "Towards Responsible Evaluation for Text-to-Speech" (2025) names "accent and gender stereotyping, as well as underrepresentation of minority speech patterns" as TTS harms and calls for benchmarks covering diverse accents, genders and languages — [arXiv 2510.06927](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06927v1) (snippet).
- Zero-shot accent synthesis "without accented data" is being pursued precisely because accented corpora are scarce and sensitive — [Accent Vector, arXiv 2603.07534](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07534) (title).

### Inferences
- A kids' math game that wants a *teen* character voice should expect hosted vendors to refuse or quietly age-up "16-year-old" prompts (ElevenLabs policy), which is itself an argument for an in-house or open-weights approach — but then the project inherits the safety burden (no watermark unless added, e.g. by reusing Chatterbox's Perth watermarker).
- The accent-stereotype risk is concrete: with sparse L2 training data a model asked for "Japanese accent" will likely produce an exaggerated caricature; a defensible design uses real L2 speaker data with consent (Common Voice contributors opted in) and measures listener perception with L1-Japanese raters rather than shipping on vibes.

### Gaps
- Hume, MiniMax, Inworld, Fish and Gradium policies on minor-sounding designed voices: not retrievable (docs blocked; search budget exhausted).
- No paper found that studies stereotyping specifically in *designed* (text-prompted) voices as opposed to preset voices.

## KQ7. Honest assessment — solved product? solved research? open research? — and what a hobbyist with one consumer GPU could realistically do in 3 and 12 months

### Takeaway
"Describe a voice in text" is a **solved product problem for adult voices with common native accents**, a **mostly solved research problem at coarse granularity** (gender, pitch, age bucket, emotion, major accents), and still **open** for fine-grained age, non-native/L2 accents, children's voices, diversity (one description → many distinct voices), and evaluation; a hobbyist cannot compete on base models (Parler-Mini alone cost ~1,150 H100-hours — estimate) but can plausibly contribute a description→speaker-embedding mapper on top of an open zero-shot cloner, or a Common-Voice-based age/accent-aware fine-tune of Parler/Qwen3-TTS, plus a missing evaluation set.

### Cited Findings
- Base-model compute: Parler-TTS Mini v1 (880M) used "4 nodes of 8 H100 80GB ... for around 1.5 days" on ~1k h LibriTTS-R + MLS English — [parler-tts training README](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts/blob/main/training/README.md). (32 GPUs × 36 h ≈ 1,150 H100-hours — my arithmetic, estimate.)
- Fine-tuning is documented and Colab-scale for Parler-TTS ("A Colab notebook demonstrates single-speaker dataset fine-tuning") — [parler-tts README](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts); ParaSpeechCaps fine-tuned parler-tts-mini-v1 on 282 h + 2,450 h and released the checkpoints — [ajd12342/paraspeechcaps](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ajd12342/paraspeechcaps/main/README.md).
- Qwen3-TTS fine-tuning currently supports only the 0.6B/1.7B **Base** models for single-speaker, full fine-tuning (batch 2, lr 2e-5, 3 epochs in the example), with a JSONL of `audio`, `text`, `ref_audio` and no description field — the VoiceDesign model has no published fine-tuning recipe — [Qwen3-TTS finetuning docs](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/tree/main/finetuning).
- Lightweight hosts for a mapper approach: Zonos (1.6B, Apache-2.0) conditions on a speaker embedding from a "ResNet293-SimAM-ASP" encoder (VoxBlink2) and runs on "6GB+ VRAM" — [Zyphra/Zonos](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Zyphra/Zonos/main/README.md); [Zonos speaker-embedding card](https://huggingface.co/Zyphra/Zonos-v0.1-speaker-embedding) (snippet); Chatterbox Turbo is 350M, MIT — [resemble-ai/chatterbox](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/main/README.md); CosyVoice 3 is 0.5B, Apache-2.0 — [FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice); Step-Audio-TTS-3B needs 8 GB — [Step-Audio README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio/main/README.md).
- Precedent for the mapper approach at small scale: Chen et al. train a LoRA-adapted LM to map impression prompts to speaker embeddings, decoupled from the TTS backbone, in both discriminative and flow-matching forms — [ISCA 2024](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/chen24q_interspeech.html) (snippet); DreamVG trains a diffusion prior over speaker embeddings from 900 annotated speakers — [DreamVoice](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16314) (snippet); ProPS (2026) generalises to embedding *distributions* — [arXiv 2607.05276](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05276); contrastive text–audio alignment variants exist (TP-CLAP, DMP-TTS) — [arXiv 2607.25085](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25085); [arXiv 2512.09504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.09504) (titles).
- Benchmarks a hobbyist can use: InstructTTSEval (EN/ZH; three tasks) — [GitHub](https://github.com/KexinHUANG19/InstructTTSEval); MINT-Bench multilingual instruction-following TTS (2026) — [arXiv 2604.17958](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17958) (title); CapSpeech's evaluation toolkit (CC BY-NC) — [GitHub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WangHelin1997/CapSpeech/main/README.md).
- Data (from KQ5): Common Voice English v27 = 2,826.8 validated h, 100,546 speakers, 64% age-labelled, CC0 — [cv-corpus-27.0 JSON](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/common-voice/cv-dataset/main/datasets/scripted-speech/cv-corpus-27.0-2026-09-11.json); Data-Speech (MIT) for captions — [GitHub](https://github.com/huggingface/dataspeech).

### Inferences (all estimates unless cited)
- **Where it is solved.** Adult, native-accent, emotion-steerable voice design with persistent identity: use Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign (Apache-2.0, 1.7B, runs on a consumer GPU) or any hosted vendor; nothing a hobbyist builds in a year will beat these on general quality.
- **Where it is open and tractable for one person.**
  1. *Controllable identity on top of a frozen cloner* ("description → speaker embedding"). Train a small text encoder (or LoRA on a 0.5–1B LLM) to output the speaker-embedding space of Zonos/Chatterbox/CosyVoice, with a flow-matching head so one prompt yields a *distribution* of voices (ProPS/Chen-style). Data: Common Voice English speakers with age + gender + accent, captions synthesised from metadata. Compute: embeddings for ~100k speakers can be extracted on one 24 GB GPU in hours; the mapper is tens of millions of parameters and trains in <1 GPU-day (estimate). This is a credible 3-month project and directly yields a "16-year-old Japanese-accented boy" knob to the extent Common Voice contains such speakers.
  2. *Age/accent-aware fine-tune of a description-conditioned TTS* (Parler-TTS Mini or, once a recipe exists, Qwen3-TTS-VD) on Common Voice + GLOBE with Data-Speech captions extended with age bucket and accent. 1.7B full fine-tuning is at the edge of a single 24 GB card; LoRA or Parler-Mini (880M) fits. Expect weeks of data cleaning (Common Voice is noisy; GLOBE exists because of that) — a 6–12-month project.
  3. *Evaluation gap.* No public benchmark measures perceived-age error or L2-accent fidelity of designed voices. A small, well-run perceptual test set (e.g. 50 prompts × 5 systems rated by L1-Japanese and child-development-aware raters, plus automatic age/accent classifiers such as Vox-Profile) would be a genuine, cheap research contribution and is the most defensible 3-month scope.
- **Where it is open and *not* tractable alone.** Children's voices (6–12) — no permissive data, and strong vendor prohibitions; genuinely new base models; multilingual accent-faithful L2 synthesis at Gradium's "100 accents" scale.
- **Realistic 3-month scope:** pick Zonos or Chatterbox as the frozen backbone; extract speaker embeddings for Common Voice English speakers with age/gender/accent labels; template-caption them (Data-Speech style) and train a text→embedding flow-matching mapper; build the perceptual + classifier evaluation; publish a model card and the eval set (CC0-derived, so shippable). Deliverable for Math City: a handful of designed character voices exported as fixed embeddings/reference clips.
- **Realistic 12-month scope:** add GLOBE + Emilia-YODAS (CC BY 4.0) for cleaner audio; LoRA-fine-tune Parler-Mini or Qwen3-TTS on captions that include age bucket + accent; run InstructTTSEval-EN and the custom age/accent set; write it up. Shipping constraint: everything used must be CC0/CC BY/Apache — which excludes ParaSpeechCaps, CapSpeech, EARS, Expresso, MyST, L2-ARCTIC, Speech Accent Archive and UME-ERJ from the shipped model (they are fine for a learning-only side branch).

### Gaps
- No published single-GPU fine-tuning time/VRAM figures for Parler-TTS, Qwen3-TTS or Zonos fine-tunes were found; estimates above are mine.
- Whether Qwen3-TTS-VoiceDesign fine-tuning (with description fields) will be released is unknown.
- Diversity/mode-collapse metrics from VoiceDesigner/ProPS (which would calibrate how hard approach 1 is) were unreachable.
